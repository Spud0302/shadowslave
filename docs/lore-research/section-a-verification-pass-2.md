### A1/A2 — Nephis had an earlier named Aspect: **[Fire Keeper]**

**CONFIDENCE: CANON (author clarification)**

The first-pass list of formal Aspect names was incomplete. Guiltythree has explicitly clarified that
**Fire Keeper was the name of Nephis's Aspect in her First Nightmare**. In the author's archived setting
notes he also describes her Aspect as having evolved **from Fire Keeper to Light Bringer**.

This gives the verified/author-confirmed named-Aspect corpus at least four entries:

1. **[Temple Slave]** — Sunny's initial First-Nightmare Aspect.
2. **[Shadow Slave]** — Sunny's evolved Divine Aspect.
3. **[Fire Keeper]** — Nephis's First-Nightmare Aspect; author-confirmed rather than shown in an on-page
   status screen that has been located so far.
4. **[Light Bringer]** — Nephis's evolved Divine Aspect.

All four are two-part title/noun-like constructions. The sample is still far too small to establish a
universal grammar, but it strengthens two observations:

- an Aspect's **name can change when the Aspect itself evolves**;
- the name is better treated as the label of a supernatural nature/archetype than as the name of one
  ability.

Do **not** infer the original rank of Fire Keeper from the name alone. The author clarification establishes
the name and later evolution, not a complete First-Nightmare status page.

### A4 — late-story correction: the Aspect and the Aspect Legacy do **not** have the same origin

**CONFIDENCE: CANON**

The first pass repeats the early explanation from Chapter 218 that **every Aspect contains an Aspect
Legacy**, with a unique condition for unlocking it. That remains important early-world knowledge, but
late material adds a necessary metaphysical distinction.

In Chapter **2902**, Cassie says she has no Aspect Legacy; Mordret says he has none either. Mordret then
makes the key distinction:

- the **Aspect itself does not come from the Nightmare Spell**;
- the **Aspect Legacy does**;
- there is a set way to unseal a particular Legacy, but the method is known to the Spell rather than to
  the person.

The cleanest reconciliation is therefore:

> **The underlying Aspect belongs to the person. The Aspect determines a corresponding latent Legacy
> pattern/possibility, but the recognized Aspect Legacy is a Spell-mediated construct that must be
> unsealed through its particular condition.**

That reconciliation is **INFERRED** from the early and late explanations. The direct statements about
origin are CANON.

This also creates a genuine open question:

> **Can a natural Awakened who is completely outside the Nightmare Spell ever obtain a true Aspect
> Legacy?**

The sources checked do not answer that. Do not assume “yes” merely because natural Awakened have
Aspects.

Sources:
- Chapter 218, **Shadow Dance** — NovelFull chapter list/text: https://novelfull.com/shadow-slave.html
- Chapter 2902 — NovelFull/WebNovel text, direct passage verified during this research pass.
- Current Aspect summary: https://shadowslave.fandom.com/wiki/Aspect

### A4 — Legacy Relics are much broader than enchanted equipment

**CONFIDENCE: CANON**

Direct later examples strengthen the first-pass conclusion considerably.

**Shadow Dance, fourth mastery:** in Chapters **1301–1302**, Sunny earns the fourth Legacy Relic and the
Spell awards him a **fragment of Shadow's Domain**. It is not an ordinary Memory, Echo, Attribute or
Aspect Ability. Later chapters continue to call it a Legacy Relic.

**General rule from Sunny's own explanation (Chapter 2334):** Legacy Relics are **usually Memories** and
more rarely Echoes, and they are expected to complement the Aspect rather than simply be “high-stat”
loot. But Sunny contrasts his own skill-based Shadow Dance with Nephis's Legacy, which is rooted in
understanding and can reward **knowledge** instead of an item.

**Nephis:** Chapters **2440** and later show branches of her Aspect Legacy granting structured knowledge
and understanding rather than conventional gear.

Therefore:

> `Legacy Relic = Memory` is false as a universal definition.

The safest broad definition is **a reward associated with progress/mastery in an Aspect Legacy**. Its
form depends on the Legacy.

Sources:
- NovelFull, Chapters 1301–1302, 1434, 2334, 2440, 2945: https://novelfull.com/shadow-slave.html
- https://shadowslave.fandom.com/wiki/Aspect

## A5 — Flaw verification upgrades

### Caster — lifespan cost

**CONFIDENCE: CANON**

Upgrade the first-pass Caster entry from WIKI.

Chapter **338** establishes Sunny's deduction that every use of Caster's Aspect Ability **shortens his
remaining lifespan**. The hourglass/sandglass charm he carries reverses or counters that physical aging
while active. After the charm is destroyed, the subsequent confrontation visibly confirms the cost as
Caster ages rapidly while forcing his ability.

Later Flaw discussion uses Caster as an example of how a Flaw can be personally cruel: fulfilling his
pride/duty through his power literally spends the life he is trying to devote to that duty.

### Morgan — everything she touches is cut

**CONFIDENCE: CANON**

Upgrade from wiki-backed to direct canon.

Chapter **2494** is especially useful because Mirage City strips supernatural powers while leaving
Flaws intact. Morgan can still cut what she touches because that effect is her **Flaw**, making an
ordinary-life curse into an advantage under those unusual rules. Earlier scenes also show clothing and
objects being damaged by her touch.

This is one of the strongest cases for the statement:

> **A Flaw can be mechanically advantageous in some contexts without ceasing to be a Flaw.**

Source:

### Effie — enhanced physical needs

**CONFIDENCE: CANON (wiki points to exact primary chapter; primary scene partially cross-checked)**

Effie's Aspect broadly enhances her physical capabilities. Her Flaw correspondingly enhances her
**physical needs** — hunger and the body's other appetites/requirements. The wiki's direct reference is
Chapter **130**, and the scene matches the later recurring characterization.

The safe causal statement is:

> Her Flaw is a **mirrored extension of the same physical nature** as her Aspect.

Do not reduce it to “she gets hungry”; hunger is simply the most visible recurring manifestation.

- **WIKI:** Ki Song's Flaw is her love for her daughters.
- **UNKNOWN:** the exact supernatural rule — whether this is an emotional compulsion, inability to bear
  children, inevitable loss, or another mechanism.

Do **not** promote community shorthand such as “she cannot have children” to CANON without a primary
passage.

## A6 — Weaver's Mask: direct reversal, not cure

**CONFIDENCE: CANON**

Remove the TODO from the first pass. Chapter **778** explicitly establishes that **Weaver's Mask can
reverse a person's Flaw**. The critical distinction is that the Flaw is not erased; its operation is
inverted.

For Sunny, the useful conceptual example is:

- normal **[Clear Conscience]**: cannot lie;
- reversed condition: truth becomes forbidden instead.

Guiltythree's archived Q&A gives illustrative reversals for other characters:

- **Nephis:** constant pain except while actively using her abilities;
- **Effie:** physical needs can never be satiated;
- **Cassie:** instead of being unable to see, she would be unable to *unsee*, with catastrophic
  consequences from visions;
- **Kai:** his truth/lie-related condition reverses in corresponding fashion.

Treat those non-Sunny examples as **CANON (author clarification)** rather than scenes that have actually
happened in the novel.

Sources:
- NovelFull, Chapter 778 **In the Shadows**: https://novelfull.com/shadow-slave.html
- Guiltythree FAQ/archive: https://shadowslave.fandom.com/wiki/Shadow_Slave_Wiki:Frequently_Asked_Questions

Conclusion remains:

> **Reversing a Flaw is not removing a Flaw.**
