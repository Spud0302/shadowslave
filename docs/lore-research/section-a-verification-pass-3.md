## A5 — Falcon Scott Flaw cluster: direct novel verification

**CONFIDENCE: CANON**

Chapter **992, The Fall of Falcon Scott (10)** directly records Sunny reviewing whether his cohort's Flaws will matter in the coming battle. The passage is especially valuable because it names or describes **five** Flaws in one place and explicitly frames them as Flaws.

### Quentin

Sunny says Quentin **seemed unable to break a promise**.

This upgrades the claim from wiki-only to direct canon, but the wording remains deliberately cautious. It does **not** prove that every promise is enforced by an absolute supernatural compulsion under all circumstances; the narrator's wording is still `seemed unable`.

**Safe statement:** Quentin's observed Flaw prevents, or at minimum strongly constrains, breaking promises.

### Kim

Sunny says Kim was **naturally unassuming** while reviewing the cohort's Flaws.

This confirms that her unassuming nature is not merely mundane appearance/personality; it is the Flaw Sunny has deduced. What remains unknown is the exact supernatural boundary: whether attention slides off her, memories of her weaken, observers undervalue her, or the effect is subtler.

**Safe statement:** Kim's Flaw manifests as supernatural or Flaw-linked unassumingness; exact mechanics remain UNKNOWN.

### Samara

Sunny says Samara had a **leisurely rate of soul essence regeneration**.

Combined with her Aspect converting/charging soul essence into kinetic energy and heat, this is a direct resource tension between power expression and recovery.

### Belle

Sunny says Belle had to work **especially hard on impulse control**.

The direct text establishes impulse control as the manifestation of the Flaw. It does not supply a formal bracketed name or a numerical/absolute compulsion rule.

### Luster

Sunny begins to describe Luster's Flaw and then effectively says it is **obvious/apparent**, without defining it.

This confirms that the ambiguity is in the novel itself. The exact mechanics should therefore remain **UNKNOWN**, not reverse-engineered from personality jokes.

### Dorn

Sunny says Dorn's was the cruelest of the cohort's Flaws: his body was **slowly but constantly growing** and would likely one day collapse under its own weight.

This is a long-horizon Flaw rather than an immediate combat penalty.

## A5 — Tristan: hot-bloodedness is directly tied to his Flaw

**CONFIDENCE: CANON for the manifestation; UNKNOWN for exact hard mechanics**

Chapter **1705, Familiar** has Cassie explain Tristan's reckless duel with Master Sunless by saying that it is his **Flaw**, then describing him as a **hot-blooded fool**. Nephis later thinks about teaching him to manage his Flaw better.

This is stronger than the wiki shorthand `Being hot-blooded`, but still does not establish a formal rune-name or an exact command such as `must challenge anyone who insults him`.

**Safe statement:** Tristan's Flaw manifests as pronounced hot-bloodedness/impulsive aggression that must be actively managed.

## A5 — Ki Song: separate a confirmed weakness from an earlier hypothesis

**CONFIDENCE: CANON for love of her daughters being her exploitable Flaw; INFERRED/UNKNOWN for reproductive mechanics**

Two pieces of evidence must not be collapsed into one claim.

### Earlier inference

Around Chapter 1944, Sunny and Cassie infer that Ki Song's Flaw is connected to **family**. Sunny wonders whether she may have been unable to have children of her own. The scene explicitly presents this as a cautious guess, not a confirmed rune description.

Therefore `Ki Song cannot have children` is **not safe as established canon** from that scene alone.

### Later battle confirmation

During the final confrontation with Nephis, the narrative identifies Ki Song's only exploitable weakness as **her daughters** and describes her love for them as her Flaw. Nephis is able to use a promise concerning their survival to force the Queen into a fatal commitment.

The exact supernatural mechanism is still not shown as a bracketed rune rule. What is confirmed is that the Flaw is genuinely tied to **her love/attachment to her daughters**.

Sources:
- https://shadowslave.fandom.com/wiki/Ki_Song
- https://shadowslave.fandom.com/wiki/Domain_War
- Chapter 1944 discussion (family hypothesis)
- Chapter 2258, **Regicide** (battle confirmation)

Notes:
- Keep `love for daughters` and `cannot bear children` as **different confidence levels**.
- The second may be part of the underlying Flaw, but current evidence checked here does not prove it.

## A5 — Anvil: his statement is canon; the fate mechanism is not

**CONFIDENCE: CANON for the Flaw as understood/stated by Anvil; UNKNOWN for whether the Flaw directly manipulates fate**

Chapter **2163, Iron Heart** reveals Anvil's Flaw: he believes that he **must lose everything he cherishes**. He blames Warden's death on it and decides the only defense is to cherish nothing and no one.

Chapter **2164, Nothing to Lose** confirms the psychological consequence. Cassie sees that Anvil was not born emotionless; he **trained himself into indifference** because he believed attachment would doom whatever he valued. That decision is then presented as the road that gradually stripped away his humanity.

What these chapters do **not** cleanly prove is that the Flaw reaches across causality and directly engineers every death/loss. The text supports the curse and supports Anvil's interpretation, but the precise causal mechanism remains unresolved.

**Safe statement:** Anvil's Flaw condemns him to lose what he cherishes; his attempt to beat it by refusing attachment becomes catastrophic. Whether the Flaw literally manipulates fate to manufacture every loss remains UNKNOWN.

# Design consequences from this verification pass

These are **design proposals**, not canon claims.

1. **A Flaw system should support multiple mechanical families instead of one generic debuff template.** Chapter 992 alone gives obligation, presentation/social, resource recovery, impulse control, deliberately hidden mechanics, and long-term physical deterioration.
2. **A Flaw does not need to be combat-negative every second.** Dorn's may take years to become fatal; Kim's may even be advantageous in some contexts; Luster's is narratively legible without being formally explained.
3. **The player should not necessarily be shown the exact rule immediately.** Canon contains Flaws that friends deduce over months and Flaws whose exact mechanics remain deliberately obscure.
4. **Behavioral Flaws should be constraints/pressures, not simple potion effects.** Quentin and Tristan are much closer to rules on choices than to `-20% attack`.
5. **Do not force every Flaw into an equal-and-opposite balance formula with its Aspect.** The examples are much more personal and asymmetric.
6. **Allow very long-horizon consequences.** A Flaw like Dorn's matters because it shapes a life, not because it lowers today's DPS.
